# Workflow - Write LinkedIn Posts for One Specific Reader

## Goal
Write LinkedIn posts that feel human, relevant, and useful by writing to one specific person profile instead of writing to "everyone".

## Inputs
- Topic or source asset (article, note, client insight, or personal observation)
- Post objective (clicks, saves, comments, or DMs)
- One real reader profile (name + role + context)
- One concrete outcome you want for that reader after reading

## Core principle
If the post is not clearly useful for one specific person, it will likely be generic for everyone.

## Reader profile card (must complete before drafting)
- Who is this post for? (one real person profile)
- What pressure are they under this week?
- What decision are they trying to make?
- What do they currently believe that may be incomplete?
- What is one useful shift this post should create?

## Steps
1. Pick one specific reader profile.
   - Prefer someone you could name from your network, clients, or ICP.
   - If needed, anchor to one person note in 01_People/ or one role in your target account list.

2. Write the post promise in one sentence.
   - Format: "After reading this, this person can ____."
   - If you cannot complete this sentence, do not draft yet.

3. Capture the real tension.
   - Write one line for what the reader says publicly.
   - Write one line for what they privately worry about.
   - Use the private worry as the emotional engine of the post.

4. Draft the opening with specificity.
   - Start with a claim, question, or observation the reader would recognize from lived experience.
   - Avoid abstract framing and broad trend language in the first 2 lines.

5. Teach one concrete thing.
   - Include one framework, one example, or one diagnostic question.
   - Make it actionable within 5-10 minutes.

6. Add a "human pass" before finalizing.
   - Remove generic phrases that sound mass-produced.
   - Replace broad words with concrete nouns, numbers, and situations.
   - Keep cadence natural: mix short and medium-length sentences.

7. Close with a reader-relevant CTA.
   - Comments CTA: ask for disagreement or sector-specific examples.
   - Click CTA: point to deeper asset with a clear reason to open it.
   - DM CTA: offer one practical template/checklist.

8. Run the audience-of-one quality gate.
   - Ask: "Would my chosen reader save this, send it, or act on it today?"
   - If no, rewrite the hook and insight before publishing.

## Outputs
- 1 LinkedIn draft aimed at one specific reader profile
- 1 first comment aligned to the post objective
- 1 short learning note: what resonated or fell flat for that profile

## Quality checklist
- [ ] One specific reader profile is defined before drafting
- [ ] One-sentence post promise is clear
- [ ] Hook reflects a real problem the reader recognizes
- [ ] Post teaches one concrete insight (not only opinion)
- [ ] Language avoids generic AI-sounding phrasing
- [ ] CTA matches the objective (click/save/comment/DM)
- [ ] Final draft passes the audience-of-one gate

## Automation opportunities
- AI drafts 3 hook variants for the same reader profile
- AI rewrites one draft into 3 tones (direct, analytical, conversational)
- AI flags vague lines and suggests more concrete alternatives
